A LOOK INSIDE THE PROCESS

The field notes.

What I explored, what I built, and what changed along the way.

NOTE 01

The project & my role

Offri is an online quotation tool developed at Slik. Businesses put proposals together from reusable, reorderable blocks, personalise them and send them to clients. I joined the Offri team during my third-year internship, working across the Vue front end and NestJS back end.

My main assignment was to research, design and implement more colour customisation. Alongside that assignment, I worked on smaller interface improvements and fixes. After my internship, I stayed at Slik part-time and continued contributing to the product.

THE DESIGN CHALLENGE

How could we give users more freedom to express their brand, while keeping Offri’s editor simple to work with?

NOTE 02

Understanding the need

The starting point was a real product need: more control over the appearance of proposals, especially text and background colours. Conversations with Thijs helped clarify what users needed and how those options should fit the existing editor.

Offri’s guiding idea, “kracht door eenvoud” — strength through simplicity — mattered here. Adding more settings would only be useful if people could understand them and reuse their choices without making every proposal from scratch.

This part of the project involved stakeholder conversations and team reviews. The task was to extend an existing product, so I also looked closely at its current styling controls and account settings.

NOTE 03

Sketches, prototypes & feedback

I started with paper sketches to explore how users could select, save and manage colours. I then developed variants in Figma, using Offri’s existing visual language rather than introducing a separate interface style.

One concrete decision came from discussing the palette structure with Thijs: instead of several small palettes with five colours each, the design moved towards one expandable palette. That gave users a single place to manage their colours.

I presented the designs during a product meeting and revisited the settings screen, which had become outdated. Reviews with Thijs and Rosanne helped refine the designs and their presentation.

A NOTE FROM THE REVIEW

The designs needed annotations so someone seeing them without my explanation could understand the interaction. That made documenting the design part of the work, too.

NOTE 04

From design to working features

The implementation connected the interface to stored styling settings. On the front end, I worked with Vue 3, TypeScript and Less. On the back end, I worked with NestJS, TypeORM and MySQL, planning new tables and columns and discussing them with Marc and Louis.

  • Text colours: colour support in the Tiptap editor and a colour picker.
  • Workspace colours: a reusable palette with named colours and hex values.
  • Block appearance: background colour, formatting and corner options, with defaults for quote and reaction blocks.
  • Reliable settings: back-end validation and default values for newly created content.

These were connected pieces of a feature: a colour selected in the interface needed to be saved, loaded again and applied consistently to the proposal.

NOTE 05

Iteration in a live product

My work also included resolving editor behaviour and styling problems, refining image block rules and adjusting PDF spacing. Working on the editor meant checking how a change affected more than the screen I was editing.

Pull-request feedback was a regular part of that process. I discussed implementation choices with the team, worked through review comments and adjusted the code. Vue and TypeScript training helped me get familiar with the stack as I took on more independent work.

NOTE 06

What I took from it

This internship let me follow a feature from an initial need through sketches, database planning and implementation. I found that I particularly enjoy the point where a design decision becomes something someone can actually use.

It also gave me experience working within someone else’s architecture, explaining my choices and improving work through feedback. Continuing part-time gave me the opportunity to build on that experience beyond the internship assignment.
