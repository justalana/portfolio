Less clothing chaos.
More room to choose.

A mobile clothing app designed around people with ADHD. The focus is on decluttering and understanding what gets worn, with a guided flow that helps users decide what to keep.

React NativeExpo RouterTypeScriptSupabase

The starting point

A crowded wardrobe can create decision fatigue. For this PLE project, I explored how a digital tool could make it easier to see what someone owns and work through clothing decisions in smaller steps.

Research & direction

I carried out a survey and two interviews with people with ADHD. The project moved towards decluttering support, with a Dutch interface and a guided sequence of choices.

The core experience

  1. Choose a weather category: warm, cold or all year.
  2. Review clothing starting with the least-worn items.
  3. Choose to keep an item or mark it for removal.
  4. See a final overview and options for donating, swapping or recycling clothing.

The wardrobe includes category and weather filters, wear logging and a clothing detail view with monthly wear statistics. Items marked for removal are collected in a separate declutter basket.

Building the app

I built the app with React Native, Expo Router and TypeScript, using Supabase for data storage. Logging a wear updates the last-worn date and wear count, and adds a wear log. I also wrote tests for weather labels, least-worn filtering and declutter selection logic.

Reflection

This project connected user research, interaction design and implementation. Its central design choice was to make decluttering a sequence of concrete decisions. The survey and interviews informed the direction; broader user testing would help evaluate how well the flow supports people in practice.
